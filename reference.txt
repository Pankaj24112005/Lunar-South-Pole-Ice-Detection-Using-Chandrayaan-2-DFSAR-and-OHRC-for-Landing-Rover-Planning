## 2. Project Objective

Develop a research workflow for analyzing lunar South Pole remote-sensing data to investigate potential water-ice-bearing regions, characterize their spatial relationships with permanently shadowed regions and lunar terrain, assess uncertainty, and eventually support resource mapping and landing-site/rover mission planning.

The project must investigate the potential contribution of the following datasets:

1. OHRC — Chandrayaan-2 Orbiter High Resolution Camera imagery and associated data products.
2. DFSAR — Chandrayaan-2 Dual Frequency Synthetic Aperture Radar data products.
3. LOLA — Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter topographic and derived products.

The final research workflow should distinguish between:
- Directly observed measurements.
- Derived image-processing products.
- Candidate water-ice indicators.
- Spatially inferred relationships.
- Scientific interpretations and hypotheses.

Do not claim that an image-processing result proves the presence of water ice. Use scientifically cautious terminology such as “potential ice-bearing area,” “candidate region,” or “ice-consistent signature” where appropriate.

### Phase 5 — Lunar ISRU Analysis, Interactive Dashboard & Mission Outputs

Develop the final interactive research dashboard and mission-oriented outputs from the validated results of the preceding phases.

The dashboard should communicate:

## 6. Phase 1 Preprocessing and Image-Processing Requirements

Develop a robust preprocessing workflow using the actual data. Choose methods according to each product's scientific properties rather than applying the same filters indiscriminately.

### A. Raster integrity and geospatial validation

- Verify that files open successfully and contain valid raster data.
- Inspect raster dimensions, resolution, band count, data types, NoData values, and georeferencing.
- Check for corrupted, truncated, empty, or incorrectly extracted files.
- Identify duplicated files and distinguish alternate versions from genuinely independent observations.
- Validate coordinate reference systems and map units.
- Detect missing coordinate metadata and flag any assumptions.
- Verify raster alignment and spatial overlap before pixelwise comparison.
- Record all relevant warnings and failures.

### B. Data quality and invalid-pixel handling

- Generate valid-data masks.
- Quantify NoData and invalid-pixel proportions.
- Identify saturation, extreme values, striping, gaps, seams, and artifacts where appropriate.
- Inspect histograms and robust descriptive statistics.
- Preserve original measurements and create derived outputs separately.
- Avoid replacing scientifically meaningful values with arbitrary constants.
- Do not silently interpolate across missing observations or invent measurements.

### C. OHRC image preprocessing

If actual OHRC image rasters are present:

- Inspect image intensity distributions and image metadata.
- Assess contrast, dynamic range, illumination variation, and potential image artifacts.
- Test suitable contrast enhancement methods, such as robust percentile stretching and CLAHE, where justified.
- Compare carefully selected denoising filters, such as median, Gaussian, or bilateral filtering, only where their use is appropriate.
- Evaluate edge-preserving enhancement for crater rims, boulders, and surface morphology.
- Preserve a minimally processed reference image for comparison.
- Record each filter's parameters and its effects on important surface features.

Do not apply aggressive denoising that erases small geological features.

If OHRC image rasters are missing, preprocess available metadata and vector catalogs as appropriate, produce a clear missing-data report, and do not claim that OHRC imagery has been processed.

### D. DFSAR radar preprocessing

First identify the exact DFSAR product type and the measurements actually available.

Determine whether the data are raw SAR, calibrated radar imagery, derived mosaics, backscatter products, polarimetric channels, or other products.

Where supported by documentation and the data:

- Check radiometric calibration and product scaling.
- Inspect radar intensity or backscatter distributions.
- Evaluate speckle reduction using suitable methods such as Lee, refined Lee, or other justified filters.
- Compare filtered and unfiltered results quantitatively and visually.
- Preserve potentially meaningful small targets and radar texture.
- Check incidence-angle effects, geometric distortions, mosaicking artifacts, and invalid regions where relevant.
- Inspect available polarization channels and their compatibility.
- Record the effect of each preprocessing operation on data statistics and spatial structure.

Do not assume that all products contain complex SAR data or support polarimetric decomposition. Do not perform phase-sensitive operations on products that lack the required complex measurements.

Do not apply optical-image enhancement techniques blindly to calibrated radar measurements.

### E. LOLA topographic preprocessing

For available LOLA-derived elevation and terrain products:

- Verify whether each raster represents elevation, slope, error, illumination, or another derived variable.
- Check vertical and horizontal units.
- Inspect elevation distributions, extreme values, missing pixels, and discontinuities.
- Evaluate DEM artifacts and local surface roughness where appropriate.
- Generate slope and terrain derivatives only when the source raster and its projection support valid calculations.
- Select an appropriate neighborhood size for terrain derivatives and explain the choice.
- Preserve elevation uncertainty or error information where available.
- Do not treat an elevation-error raster as an elevation raster.
- Do not interpret a dark image or a low-elevation value as proof of permanent shadow or water ice.

For PSR products, verify the scientific definition, thresholding method, source, and spatial resolution before interpreting their classifications.

### F. Filter and enhancement comparison

Where multiple preprocessing options are scientifically appropriate, compare them rather than choosing a method arbitrarily.

For each relevant data type, evaluate:
- Original versus processed appearance.
- Signal and noise characteristics.
- Preservation of boundaries and small features.
- Changes in descriptive statistics.
- Valid-data coverage before and after processing.
- Processing time and memory use.
- Suitability for the subsequent scientific task.

Use quantitative metrics only when their assumptions are satisfied. For example, do not report PSNR or SSIM as measures of scientific correctness when no suitable reference image exists.

Select the final preprocessing configuration based on documented evidence and explain its limitations.
